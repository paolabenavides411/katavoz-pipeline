# Harvested Vocabulary Bank

**Source:** real multiple-choice vocabulary/spelling/proofreading/definition-matching quiz results from the user's own court-reporting coursework (Google Drive, "TRI CLASS RECORDINGS" area → Term 1: Legal 1, English 1, Medical 1; Term 2: Advanced English 2, Medical Terminology & Anatomy 2). Harvested 2026-09-30.

**Why this exists:** the user's stated rationale — these are real words a court reporter will actually hear in a deposition or on the record. They are uncommon in everyday English, but common in legal/medical testimony. If a student has never encountered a word before, they can't tell whether what they heard was a real word or a mishearing/mistranscription. Dictation transcripts should draw on this bank so students meet these words in context before they meet them cold in a real deposition.

**Extraction rules used** (per the user's explicit instructions):
- Plain vocabulary MC quizzes (a target word tested against multiple-choice synonyms) → the target word itself, plus non-trivial distractor words from the same question.
- "Pick the misspelled word" / proofreading quizzes → the CORRECTLY SPELLED form only, never the misspelled form.
- Definition/synonym-matching quizzes (e.g. medical term matched to a plain-English definition) → the actual technical term, not its plain-English definition.

**How to use this when building/revising a dictation**: pick words from the relevant subject section(s) that fit the scenario's fact pattern naturally (don't force-fit). A single dictation won't use more than a handful of these — this file is a reservoir to draw from across all current and future dictations, not a per-dictation checklist. Mark off or note in the per-dictation build notes which words from here were actually used, to spread coverage across dictations over time rather than reusing the same handful repeatedly.

**Status**: raw harvest, not yet deduplicated across the 5 source courses (English 1 and Advanced English 2 vocabulary sections in particular overlap somewhat — e.g. "dilettante," "commiserate," "nebulous," "recidivist" appear in both). Not a blocker for use — duplication across sections just means a word is extra well-attested as something students were actually tested on.
